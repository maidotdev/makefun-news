World news, 11 November 2026.

Abu Dhabi talks, first in nine months, ended with a prisoner exchange in principle, not on land or a ceasefire. Kushner and Witkoff will meet Putin, then Zelenskyy (NBC, Al Jazeera).

In Antalya, Guterres urged fossil-fuel taxes and a COP31 climate-justice compact; Turkey sought 35% of final energy from electricity by 2035; U.S. absent (UN News, AP, Reuters).

Iran's army said a tanker caught fire after hitting a mine in Hormuz; a Guard claim of two mine hits was unconfirmed (Reuters).

Congo reported 11,042 confirmed Ebola cases and 5,387 deaths from Bundibugyo, no vaccine approved, in 8 provinces (Reuters).

Israeli strikes killed three in Gaza, including the Al-Absi brothers; the army said it hit militants. Since the Oct. 2025 ceasefire, 1,622 have died (Reuters, Haaretz).

The U.S. charged al-Saadi, Mohajeri and al-Hassani over nearly 20 alleged Iran-linked attacks and a September plot at RAF Fairford; Iran denied them (BBC).

The House voted 211-204 to refer Pete Hegseth's impeachment to committee; Thomas Massie cast the only Republican no, and 18 did not vote (House clerk, NPR).

AP called Democrat Scott Colom over Cindy Hyde-Smith in Mississippi, its first Black senator since Reconstruction, Democrats' eighth flip, to at least 55-44 (AP, CBS News).

October U.S. CPI rose 0.4%, above 0.3% expected, and core CPI 0.3%; December Fed-hike odds neared 90%, and Asian markets were mixed (NDTV Profit).

Haiti's Fils-Aimé praised Bukele's claimed strike on the alleged 400 Mawozo base; the BBC could not verify reports of up to 20 dead on Sunday.

Judge Kuntz let Trump's $10 billion BBC defamation suit proceed over an edited 6 January 2021 speech, without ruling on the merits (BBC).

A Seongnam bus hit 32 vehicles at 7:34 a.m., injuring 57, 9 seriously; the driver said the brakes failed (Yonhap).

Britain held two minutes' silence at 11:00 for the 1918 Armistice, and over 35,000 gathered in Ottawa (BBC, Royal Canadian Legion).
